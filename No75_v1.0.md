# A Star-Forest and Fibonacci-Recurrence Proof of the Edge General Position Conjecture for Fibonacci Cubes

Ryutaro Yonezu

Independent Researcher

Version 1.0 — 2026-11-19

## Abstract

We prove that the edge general position number of the Fibonacci cube of dimension n is 2F_n for every n >= 2. The union of the two boundary coordinate classes is the unique maximum edge general position set. Star-forest and projection arguments reduce the upper bound to two explicit finite families of strict integer inequalities; a recurrence carries these bases to all dimensions. The accompanying Python certificate checks the finite bases and the small equality cases.

## Theorem

For every integer n >= 2, the edge general position number of the Fibonacci cube Gamma_n is 2 F_n. Furthermore the unique maximum edge general position set is the union of the two boundary coordinate classes, Theta_1 and Theta_n. Here F_0=0, F_1=1, F_{r+2}=F_{r+1}+F_r.

The proof is analytical except for two explicit finite families of integer inequalities and the small equality classification, which are verified by `verify_v010.py` using Python standard-library integer arithmetic. The infinite range is reduced to finite bases by a separate proof of the recurrence; it is not extrapolated from finitely many dimensions.

## 1. Model and known lower bound

Gamma_n has as vertices binary words of length n with no adjacent ones, and an edge changes exactly one coordinate, its color. Hamming distance equals graph distance: remove ones not present in the target and then insert target ones not present in the source, preserving legality throughout. Hence any walk changing distinct coordinates is a geodesic, and every geodesic changes distinct coordinates. An edge general position set S contains at most two edges on every geodesic. The number of legal words is F_{n+2} by the usual first-bit recurrence.

The number of color-i edges is F_i F_{n-i+1}: the two endpoints differ at i, all existing immediate neighbors of i must be zero, and the unrestricted legal prefix/suffix give these factors (including the empty side at a boundary). The Fibonacci addition identity gives

    F_n = F_i F_{n-i+1} + F_{i-1} F_{n-i}.

For example, this identity follows by induction in n-i, holding for n=i and n=i+1. Thus each class has at most F_n edges, with strict inequality for 1<i<n. The two boundary classes together form an edge general position set since a geodesic changes each coordinate at most once. This is the lower bound already established in Klavzar–Tan (2023), Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 4.3. A set using no more than two colors consequently has size at most 2F_n, with equality only for the boundary union.

## 2. Star-forest lemma (isometric subgraphs of hypercubes)

Suppose S uses at least three distinct colors. The graph with edges S and their incident vertices is a disjoint union of nonempty stars.

Proof. If this graph has a simple path of three edges, its color sequence either uses three distinct colors, which is a forbidden geodesic, or is i,j,i with i!=j (consecutive edges in a simple path cannot have the same color). In the latter case the four vertices of that path have all four assignments on i,j and share all other coordinates. The two length-two subpaths, taken in either direction, have every one of these four vertices as a possible ending vertex. Choose any selected edge f of a third color k, which is available by the hypothesis. The fixed i,j bits of f match some ending vertex z of an oriented length-two subpath. The subpath changes i,j only, so all four path vertices have a common k bit. Choose the endpoint y of f with that k bit. A shortest z-to-y path changes no i,j,k coordinate because the endpoints agree there. Concatenate the selected two-edge subpath, this shortest path, and f. The concatenation changes distinct coordinates, so it is a geodesic containing three selected edges, a contradiction. This also applies if z=y.

There is thus no simple path of length three in the selected-edge graph. It is bipartite. In a connected nonempty component, two adjacent vertices both of degree at least two would give a simple three-edge path by choosing an extra neighbor on each side (the extra neighbors are distinct by bipartiteness). If there were two nonadjacent vertices of degree at least two, a shortest connecting path would have adjacent vertices of degree at least two. Hence there is at most one vertex of degree at least two in each component, which is a star (including one-edge components). QED.

If the maximum star degree is Delta, and U denotes the vertices incident to S, then

    |S| <= Delta/(Delta+1) |U|,      |S| < |U|  (S nonempty).

Both follow by summing the identities for a d-edge star: d edges, d+1 vertices. In particular Delta <=3 gives |S| <= 3 F_{n+2}/4. For n>=5, F_{n-1}<2F_n/3 follows from F_{n-3}<F_{n-2}; therefore F_{n+2}=2F_n+F_{n-1}<8F_n/3, and |S|<2F_n. For n=3,4 the non-strict bound 3 F_{n+2}/4 <= 2F_n holds; n=2 cannot use three colors. Small equality cases are handled separately below.

## 3. Star-projection exclusion lemma

Suppose a selected star has center v and at least d arms, where d>=3. Choose any d of its colors, a set I={i_1<...<i_d}. For any selected edge f of color k, its fixed bits on I excluding k have either all the same relative disagreements with v (all zero), or all opposite (all one).

Proof. Otherwise choose i,j in I excluding k such that f disagrees with v in exactly one of these two positions. The two selected star arms of colors i,j form the path v^i--v--v^j (superscripts mean flipping the designated bit). Orient this two-edge path so its final endpoint z has the fixed i,j bits of f; the disagreement hypothesis guarantees one of v^i,v^j does. Take the endpoint y of f whose k bit agrees with z. The shortest z-to-y path changes none of i,j,k. As in Section 2, concatenate that selected two-edge path, the shortest connector, and f to obtain a geodesic with three selected edges. Contradiction. QED.

Consequently every vertex incident to S has a projection on I at Hamming distance at most one from v restricted to I or from its bitwise complement. Indeed, outside color k the relative disagreements are constant, and at k at most one bit can differ from this constant. Write D(n,I,b) for the number of legal words whose projection on I has Hamming distance h from b=v|_I satisfying 2<=h<=d-2. All of these words are excluded from U. Thus

    |U| <= F_{n+2} - D(n,I,b).

Adjacent chosen coordinates i_j,i_{j+1} must both be zero in b: if either center bit were one, the star arm changing its chosen neighbor would be illegal (and both ones is already illegal). This is the only feasibility restriction on b for some star with chosen arms I, because setting every other coordinate to zero gives a legal center and all d legal single flips. We only need necessity for the proof.

## 4. Exact counting formula and finite-base reduction

Let L=i_1-1, R=n-i_d, and g_j=i_{j+1}-i_j>=1. Then n=L+R+1+sum_j g_j. For a fixed projection a in {0,1}^d, the number N(a) of legal words with those bits is

    N(a) = F_{L+2-a_1} F_{R+2-a_d} product_{j=1..d-1} F_{g_j+1-a_j-a_{j+1}}.

To prove this, the initial/free left segment contributes F_{L+2} if a_1=0 and F_{L+1} if a_1=1; this holds also at L=0, since F_1=F_2=1. The right segment is identical. Between two fixed bits x,y distance g apart, the number of assignments is (T^g)_{xy} where T=[[1,1],[1,0]] is the allowed adjacent-bit transition matrix. By elementary matrix induction, T^g=[[F_{g+1},F_g],[F_g,F_{g-1}]] for g>=1, including F_0=0 for adjacent ones. Multiplication counts independent intervals between fixed bits. Therefore

    D = sum_{a: 2<=Hamming(a,b)<=d-2} N(a).

We will establish two inequalities for all admissible choices of L,R,g,b:

    d=4:  M_4 = 5 F_n - 2(F_{n+2}-D) > 0;
    d=5:  M_5 = D - F_{n-1} > 0.

Fix d and b. For L>=2, holding the other parameters fixed, each summand of D satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence in L, because only its factor F_{L+2-a_1} depends on L. Thus D and each margin M_d satisfy that recurrence in L. The same holds for R>=2. For any one g_j>=4, holding other parameters fixed, the single gap factor satisfies the recurrence in g_j; F_n, F_{n+2}, and F_{n-1} do too. Hence M_d also satisfies this recurrence in g_j. The smaller gap arguments g_j-1 and g_j-2 are at least 2, so the center feasibility restriction does not change. Gap 1 is a separate base value and is never needed in the recurrence for gaps >=4. Center feasibility is independent of L and R. These facts permit strong induction on L+R+sum_j g_j: if a parameter lies outside its base range, the two smaller tuples are admissible and the margin is the sum of their strictly positive margins. Therefore it suffices to check exactly the following finite set of tuples:

    L,R in {0,1}; each g_j in {1,2,3}; b in {0,1}^d,
    excluding b unless all adjacent chosen positions have both center bits zero.

These sets have 748 admissible tuples for d=4 and 3412 for d=5. The maximum n in the first family is 12, and in the second it is 15. Exact checks give minimum M_4=5 and minimum M_5=4. `verify_v010.py` calculates all counts by the Fibonacci product formula, compares every projection count to a separate literal enumeration of all legal words at each base geometry, and checks feasibility by actual single-bit flips of a candidate center, not just by the adjacent-position condition. It then checks strict positivity and the stated totals. The recurrences above, not the finite verification by itself, establish the inequalities for arbitrary n.

## 5. Completing the upper bound and equality classification

Take any S. If at most two colors are used, Section 1 establishes the upper bound and equality classification. Otherwise Section 2 gives a star forest. If Delta<=3, its size is strictly below 2F_n for n>=5 and at most 2F_n for n=3,4. If Delta=4, choose its four arms in Section 3. By the first certified inequality in Section 4,

    |S| <= (4/5)|U| <= (4/5)(F_{n+2}-D) < 2 F_n.

If Delta>=5, choose five arms of any maximum-degree star, and use the second inequality. It gives

    |S| < |U| <= F_{n+2}-D < F_{n+2}-F_{n-1} = 2 F_n.

These four cases exhaust all possibilities. For n>=5 equality is possible only with at most two colors, and hence only for the full boundary union. For n=2,3,4 independent finite enumeration (BFS distances, every shortest path, every edge subset) checks that the boundary union is also the unique maximum. This proves the theorem subject to independent audit of the mathematical reasoning and finite certificate. QED.

## 6. Scope, verification and references

The direct theorem does not assert that fractional covers of cost F_n, or exact geodesic partitions, exist for all n. Earlier endpoint-capacity obstructions are not used. The analytical star lemmas concern isometric subgraphs of hypercubes, while the counting step is specific to Fibonacci cubes. No extension to all partial cubes or Lucas cubes is claimed. The known lower witness and coordinate size formula are not claimed new.
